What I do

I'm an applied ML engineer with a research lean. I ship production systems where the model is one part of a longer pipeline — somebody operates the output, somebody downstream has to act on it. The interesting work is almost never the model itself; it's how the noisy input becomes a small, scored, defensible recommendation that a clinician, on-call engineer, or trader can move on inside a few seconds.

That shape of work has its own name on this site: Signal → Rank → Explain. The methodology page spells out what the three stages mean and why every project in selected work takes the same shape, even when the domain (psychiatric medications, options pricing, incident triage, lakehouse demand) couldn't look more different.

How I work

Inverted pipelines, not firehoses. Most systems evaluate everything and try to sort the noise afterward. The work I'm proud of inverts that — cheap deterministic filters first, expensive evaluation only on what survives, decision-ready output at the end.

Scores that map to action. AUC, P(profit), expected value, similarity rank — pick the metric that the operator on the other end of the pipeline can act on. Skip the ones that are easy to measure but don't move a decision.

Explainability is part of the product. SHAP per-patient for clinicians. The setup name that put a ticker on the list, for traders. Cited prior incidents in an RCA draft, for on-call. The output isn't a number — it's a defensible recommendation.

Ship the artifact, not the framework. The deliverable is the thing somebody actually uses. The framework around it lives in the README, not in a fifteen-page architecture document.
